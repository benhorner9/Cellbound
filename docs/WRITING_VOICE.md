# Cellbound writing guide

Cellbound is a guild-management RPG. It should sound like a game made by people who understand the systems and the setting, not a fantasy game advert.

## Interface writing

Tell players what they can do, why a choice matters mechanically and what happens next. Prefer “Switch targets to stop the cast” over “Take command of the battle.” Use everyday UK English and short sentences, especially on iPad screens. Preserve established terms: Guild, Guild Bank, Tank, Healer, Damage, Cell Shock, Quest Journal, Combat Reborn, Heroic and Cellbound+.

**Combat:** tell the player which mechanic matters and what to do. **Professions:** show ingredients, costs, time and what is produced. **Trading:** show conditions, charges, currency and binding. **Menus:** use the actual feature name, not a grand label.

## Characters and lore

NPCs are allowed to sound different from one another. Elara can be direct and occasionally impatient; Silas should have his own interests. Let them argue, hesitate, mislead or joke rather than narrate a tutorial. Do not strip out specific in-world names, memorable scene titles or story reveals.

## Avoid

- “Your journey begins”, “the world awaits”, “the road ahead”, “forge your destiny”, “every decision matters”.
- “Not just X, but Y” and other generic contrasts.
- Three-part slogans with no mechanical or narrative meaning.
- “Command centre”, “hub”, “experience”, “legendary”, “epic” or “journey” used only to make ordinary features sound important.
- Technical implementation claims in UI copy, including shared-engine details.
- Pretending gameplay changed when only the description did.

## Review each new screen

1. What does the player do here?
2. What will change if they press this button?
3. Does the help text match the current gameplay rules?
4. Does any character actually sound like a person?
5. Could the same sentence appear in any fantasy game? If yes, write something specific to Cellbound.

The staging build checks only the most blatant stock marketing phrases. Human editorial review still matters.
